Data Professional Survey Breakdown
1. Project Title / Headline
📊 Data Professional Survey Breakdown Dashboard
An interactive Power BI dashboard that explores survey responses from data professionals, highlighting salary trends, job roles, programming-language preferences, demographics, career-entry challenges, and workplace satisfaction.

2. Short Description / Purpose
This dashboard analyzes responses from 630 data professionals to provide insights into salaries, job titles, countries, programming languages, and employee satisfaction. It helps users understand career patterns and perceptions across the data industry.

3. Tech Stack
The dashboard was built using:
📊 Power BI Desktop – Dashboard development and visualization
📂 Power Query – Data cleaning and transformation
🧠 DAX – Measures, calculations, and KPIs
📝 Data Modeling – Structured analysis of survey responses
📁 File Formats – .pbix for the Power BI report and .png for the dashboard preview

4. Data Source
The dashboard uses a survey dataset collected from GitHub.

The dataset includes information related to:
Job title
Salary
Country
Age
Favorite programming language
Difficulty of entering the data profession
Work/life balance satisfaction
Salary satisfaction

5. Features / Highlights
Business Problem
Data professionals and career analysts often need a simple way to understand salary differences, technology preferences, career-entry challenges, and workplace satisfaction. Raw survey data makes it difficult to identify these trends quickly.

Goal of the Dashboard

The dashboard was designed to:
Analyze salary differences across job roles
Identify the most popular programming languages
Understand the geographic distribution of respondents
Examine perceptions of entering the data profession
Measure satisfaction with work/life balance and salary
Present survey insights in an interactive and accessible format

Walkthrough of Key Visuals
Key KPIs:
Total survey participants: 630
Average age of survey takers: 29.87 years
Average happiness with work/life balance: 5.74 out of 10
Average happiness with salary: 4.27 out of 10

Average Salary by Job Title

Students, people looking for work, or respondents without a specified role

Data Scientist appears as the highest-paid role in the survey, while students and respondents without a defined role report the lowest average salary.


Country of Survey Takers
A treemap displays the geographic distribution of respondents. The largest respondent groups come from:
United States
Other countries
India
United Kingdom
Canada

This visual helps identify where the survey participants are located.

Favorite Programming Language
A stacked column chart shows programming-language preferences. Python is the dominant language, followed by R and other programming languages. C/C++, JavaScript, and Java represent smaller portions of the responses.


Difficulty to Break into Data
A donut chart categorizes respondents’ perceptions of entering the data profession:

Neither easy nor difficult: 269 respondents
Difficult: 156 respondents
Easy: 134 respondents
Very difficult: 44 respondents
Very easy: 27 respondents

The largest group considers entering the data field neither easy nor difficult, while more respondents describe the process as difficult than easy.
Happiness with Work/Life Balance

A gauge visual displays an average satisfaction score of 5.74 out of 10, indicating a moderate level of satisfaction with work/life balance.

Happiness with Salary

A second gauge shows an average salary satisfaction score of 4.27 out of 10, suggesting that respondents are less satisfied with salary than with work/life balance.
Business Impact & Insights

Career Planning: Helps aspiring professionals understand salary differences between data roles.
Skills Development: Highlights Python as the most widely preferred programming language.
Workforce Analysis: Provides insights into the geographic and demographic profile of data professionals.
Employee Experience: Reveals moderate work/life satisfaction and comparatively lower salary satisfaction.
Education and Training: Supports training providers in designing programs aligned with industry preferences.
Recruitment Strategy: Helps organizations understand the skills and expectations of data professionals.
